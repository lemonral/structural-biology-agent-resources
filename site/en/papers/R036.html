MetaAgent

MetaAgent: Automatically Constructing Multi-Agent Systems Based on Finite State Machines

First public: 2025-07 · Conference publication · ICML 2025 · Main conference

MetaAgent generates a finite-state machine containing roles, condition validators and listeners, then merges redundant states. Explicit transitions support local revision and backtracking during multi-agent execution.

Research problemMechanismEvidenceLimitsImplementationReproductionReading connectionsSources

Research problem

Automatically constructed role systems can acquire unnecessary handoffs while leaving the conditions for progress unclear.

How the mechanism works

  1. Construct roles and states from the task, with instructions, transition validators and designated message listeners.
  2. Use an adaptation model to compare and merge insufficiently distinct states or roles until the structure stabilizes.
  3. Evaluate transition conditions after execution; remain in a state to revise or return to earlier states until completion or an interaction limit.

Study setting and reported evidence

Creative writing, GPQA, ML Bench and software tasks compare prompting and manually designed systems, with tool/backtracking/optimization ablations. The automatic system does not consistently beat specialist human-designed ML systems.

Limits and interpretation

Visible FSM structure does not make model-interpreted transition conditions correct. Apparently similar states can carry different scientific preconditions, so fewer states are not automatically better.

Implications for a structural-biology agent

Editorial design inference, distinct from the paper's measured result.

Join state instructions to explicit scientific input/output contracts and deterministic checks. Preserve distinct operations when chains, units or artifact conditions differ, regardless of apparent textual similarity.

Reproduction and implementation checks

Save generated FSMs before and after merging, validator decisions and backtracking events. Compare component removals with fixed tools and budgets, and independently test transition conditions.

Reading connections

Use the paired mechanisms to locate what changes between systems: information access, control, memory, training, execution or acceptance.
