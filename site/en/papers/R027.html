R027 · Evaluation, security and research maps

MAST

Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?

First public: 2025-03-17 · Conference publication · NeurIPS 2025 · Datasets & Benchmarks

MAST derives a taxonomy of multi-agent failures from execution traces and supplies annotations and an automatic labeling workflow. It is a diagnostic resource rather than an agent that automatically repairs every failure.

Research problem

A final failed answer gives little guidance about whether the root problem was system design, coordination or task verification.

How the mechanism works

  1. Analyze traces with expert input to identify recurrent patterns before scaling annotation.
  2. Organize fourteen failure modes into system design, inter-agent misalignment and task verification, retaining the context and stage of each failure.
  3. Assess human agreement and use an LLM labeling process to expand analysis across more trajectories.

Study setting and reported evidence

The study begins with 150 manually analyzed traces and covers seven frameworks and over 1,600 trajectories overall. Agreement and observed failure distributions support the taxonomy within the sampled systems.

Limits and interpretation

A label does not establish the initial causal fault: several failures may co-occur. Framework, task and model selection determine frequencies, which are not universal production failure probabilities.

Implications for a structural-biology agent

Editorial design inference, distinct from the paper's measured result.

Log object identity, handoffs and validation events so scientific failures can be separated from planning and execution errors. Choose recovery actions through controlled interventions rather than taxonomy labels alone.

Reproduction and implementation checks

Retain annotator instructions, agreement measures and automatically labeled cases. Test whether changing the suspected failure point changes outcomes, and report disagreements or overlapping diagnoses.

Reading connections

Use the paired mechanisms to locate what changes between systems: information access, control, memory, training, execution or acceptance.

AgentDebug profiles memory, planning, reflection and action errors, identifies a consequential step and supplies targeted feedback for re-execution. Its counterfactual definition of a critical error must be distinguished from an implemented model-based diagnosis.
