PaperBench

PaperBench: Evaluating AI's Ability to Replicate AI Research

First public: Not recorded in source index · See publication record · See publication record

PaperBench asks agents to implement and run research from papers, grading partial replication with author-informed hierarchical rubrics. Code quality, real execution and result matching are distinct evidence levels.

Research problemMechanismEvidenceLimitsImplementationReproductionReading connectionsSources

Research problem

Plausible source code or old logs do not demonstrate that a research experiment runs and reproduces the target result.

How the mechanism works

  1. Use weighted pass/fail rubric leaves for development, execution and result matching, each inspecting the appropriate evidence.
  2. Copy the final submission into a clean Ubuntu/A10 environment and rerun its entry point to obtain new artifacts and logs.
  3. Apply a file-selecting model judge per rubric leaf and separately calibrate judging against human-labeled partial replications through JudgeEval.

Study setting and reported evidence

Twenty papers with three runs compare scaffolds and models; changing the scaffold helps some models and hurts another. Code-Dev scores correlate only weakly with full replication scores, and automatic judging remains imperfect.

Source coverage: Original guide checked against the full text. This English edition translates the archived review and does not claim a new independent reproduction.

Limits and interpretation

Weighted partial progress is not the fraction of fully replicated papers. Rubric weights, limited human comparisons, selection rules and possible pretraining exposure constrain broad claims.

Implications for a structural-biology agent

Editorial design inference, distinct from the paper's measured result.

Score script correctness, tool execution and numerical reproduction as separate structural-workflow nodes. Independently rerun clean environments and expose missing stages instead of hiding them behind artifact counts.

Reproduction and implementation checks

Retain all repeats, entry points, clean-run logs, rubric weights and judge calibration. Do not rank Code-Dev and full-task scores as though they measured the same outcome.

Reading connections

Use the paired mechanisms to locate what changes between systems: information access, control, memory, training, execution or acceptance.
