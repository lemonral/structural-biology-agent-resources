R047 · Learning, memory and system optimization

ToolOrchestra

ToolOrchestra: Elevating Intelligence via Efficient Model and Tool Orchestration

First public: 2025-11-26 · Conference publication · ICML 2026 · Main conference

ToolOrchestra trains a small orchestrator to choose among conventional tools and other language models. Its objective combines task outcome, cost, latency and user preferences, making resource allocation the central mechanism.

Research problemMechanismEvidenceLimitsImplementationReproductionReading connectionsSources

Research problem

Different tasks need different capabilities, but always invoking the strongest model or a fixed tool sequence wastes resources.

How the mechanism works

  1. Expose models and tools through typed calls; describe model capabilities from example trajectories rather than fixed size-based rankings.
  2. Combine normalized correctness, dollar cost, elapsed time and preference rewards, with unsuccessful trajectories gated to zero, then optimize using GRPO.
  3. Train across heterogeneous environments and randomized tool subsets and prices, including synthetic ToolScale tasks.

Study setting and reported evidence

Comparisons control ordinary/model tool access and assess benchmark accuracy with resource use. Tests with unseen replacement models retain some benefits but also show declines: transfer is not lossless.

Limits and interpretation

The system's score belongs to the whole tool/model combination, not the small controller's standalone knowledge. Prices, caches and short simulated tasks do not represent all long scientific-job costs.

Implications for a structural-biology agent

Editorial design inference, distinct from the paper's measured result.

Differentiate quick checks, expensive computation and interpretation models. Include actual GPU use, queue time and recovery in resource objectives while preserving independent scientific acceptance.

Reproduction and implementation checks

Match available experts, descriptions and price conditions across controllers. Record all delegated model calls and distinguish training cost, inference price and wall-clock latency.

Reading connections

Use the paired mechanisms to locate what changes between systems: information access, control, memory, training, execution or acceptance.
